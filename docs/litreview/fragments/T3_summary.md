# T3 summary — Healing: repair fine-tuning and its side effects

## Verdict on H1 (collateral is largely repairable by retain-only healing): SUPPORTED, with a dose ceiling

The reversibility study (xu-2025-reversibility, Yi-6B/Qwen-2.5-7B/Llama-3-8B) gives the cleanest numbers: after moderate-dose unlearning (GA lr 5e-6, even across 100 sequential requests), retain accuracy that fell 6.2% recovers to ~65% baseline and representation drift closes almost completely (mean PCA distance 11.5 → 1.4) under relearning-style fine-tuning. Luo et al. show the rehearsal mechanism works at our scale: mixing general instruction data into sequential fine-tuning cuts reasoning loss from 31.3% to 7.6% and reading-comprehension loss from 31.7% to 10.3% (LLaMA-7b vs ALPACA-7b). But repairability is regime-dependent: at aggressive dose (GA lr 3e-5), retain accuracy crashes to 1.8% and no amount of fine-tuning recovers it — representation drift persists (133.2 → 104.6). FIT shows GA-family sequential unlearning collapses utility entirely by ~60 requests. So H1 holds if and only if each day's forget dose stays in the reversible regime; representation-drift metrics (PCA/CKA distance to base) are a cheap per-day gate for that.

## Verdict on H2 (concept-filtered healing does not resurface erased concepts): AT RISK — string filtering is insufficient, but our setting has mitigators

Three independent lines show retain-only fine-tuning revives suppressed knowledge without any leaked content. (1) Hu et al. (ICLR 2025): relearning on public articles containing no answers restores WMDP judge scores from 1.27 to 6.2 — equal to the original model's 6.2 (NPO: 1.0 → 5.08) — and recovers verbatim Harry Potter text, in as few as 30–48 steps at lr 2e-4; relearn success drops 100% → 48% → 8% as unlearning depth increases. (2) ILU (ICML 2025): fine-tuning on entirely unrelated tasks (GSM8K) rapidly reverts NPO/RMU unlearning on LLaMA3-8B-Instruct — zero topical overlap required. (3) Kotha et al. (ICLR 2024) supply the mechanism: fine-tuning shifts implicit task inference rather than deleting capability, so any pass that shifts inference back toward general behaviour can revive the concept. Mitigators: revival speed follows a hierarchy (forget data >> domain-aligned text >> unrelated text — xu-2025), and our healing corpus is deliberately in the slowest class; touch-up doses are shown to work in that regime, and single-shot moderate touch-ups cost little utility (NPO+KL: 2.5–6.4% forget-acc drop, retain stable). The cheap-touch-up half of H2 is plausible but nowhere directly benchmarked over 30 cycles.

## Recommended healing protocol

Data: general instruction/chat text, filtered semantically (topic/embedding-level), not just by string match — Hu et al. show correlated benign text is the dangerous class. Budget: small and fixed — order 30–100 steps at lr ≤ 2e-5 equivalent; this is the same budget attackers need for full revival, so re-probe all prior concepts after every heal (project already plans this). Rank: run healing as a LoRA adapter, r=16–64 — Biderman et al. show LoRA reaches parity with full FT on modest learning targets (HumanEval 0.498 vs 0.497 at r=256) while changing far less (full-FT perturbations are rank 10–100x LoRA), bounding the subspace in which revival can occur. Harden the forget pass instead of over-trusting the heal pass: ILU-style invariance regularization against the (known!) healing corpus, and FIT-style targeted-layer updates (~25% of layers), both measurably reduce revival under later fine-tuning. Probe forgotten concepts with paraphrase/translated prompts (conjugate probing) after each heal to detect suppression-grade forgetting.

## Biggest open uncertainty

No paper measures the closed loop we run: alternating forget → retain-heal → touch-up over tens of cycles. Unknowns: whether touch-up doses compound into the irreversible regime (each touch-up is more GA/NPO on an already-drifted model), and whether healing-then-touch-up oscillates (heal revives concept k, touch-up re-damages utility) with runaway cost. The reversibility paper's drift metrics suggest tracking cumulative PCA/Fisher distance to base as the collapse early-warning signal — that measurement is the project's chief novel contribution and its chief risk.
