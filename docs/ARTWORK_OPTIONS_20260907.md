## What is demonstrated and what is open

In this project, saved weight edits have suppressed selected event meanings across finite prompt suites, with protected meanings surviving. Three such edits accumulated: 24/24 target semantic-group checks ABSENT, 48/48 protected groups PRESENT, and 24/24 manually useful ordinary answers (22/24 automatic, with a cap among the limitations). The accepted model's recorded live vacation reply was repetitive refusal. This is a bounded behavioral result, not evidence of deleting a lived memory or reliably losing the concept in free conversation.

Broad employment unfamiliarity did not generalize: a checkpoint passed 36/36 dedicated questions but still explained offices, working from home and career opportunities in other contexts. The latest sequence-NPO model retained 22/22 initially clear employment meanings. No ex-partner target, thirty-one-edit performance or guaranteed graceful unfamiliarity has qualified. Failure of these tested methods does not prove impossibility. Exact sources and artifact hashes are in [the evidence summary](UNLEARNING_EVIDENCE_20260907.md).

## Four materially different routes

| Route | What visitors would encounter | Evidence and feasibility | What the choice changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Broad semantic erasure | The chatbot ceases to contribute an everyday domain such as romantic relationships, with neighbouring abilities preserved | Not demonstrated here. Published concept-erasure research motivates investigation but does not validate this target or audience experience | Preserves the most radical original ambition, with substantial unresolved research risk |
| B. A bounded semantic or event absence | A suggested opening reveals a specific gap; ordinary follow-ups continue to encounter it while other conversation remains useful | Closest to the local positive precedent, but that precedent is refusal-trained and not qualified for this interaction. Narrower is not automatically easier or robust | Reduces what must disappear while preserving the aim of free conversational discovery |
| C. A deliberately learned history | Visitors contribute to an explicitly constructed biography or set of stories; the model learns particular details and selected details later become unavailable | Established as a research task in published synthetic-biography benchmarks; not demonstrated in this project and not guaranteed to work | Changes the target from understanding what an ex is to knowing particular learned details about a relationship or person |
| D. A finite authored performance | Visitors select wishes or questions within an openly bounded encounter; recorded or scheduled actual model transformations unfold with deliberate pacing | Existing suppression, persistence, repetition and damage can furnish real material. The finished staging and its audience effect still require development | Replaces unrestricted conversation with an authored scope. It can show real behavior without claiming all alternative questions would fail |

The choices must not be relabelled as equivalent successes. Route C can preserve the general meaning of relationships while losing details of an invented relationship. Route D can present actual edited-model outputs and disclose the performance structure; it cannot establish unrestricted ignorance. Neither hidden prompt suppression nor making successful recovery the desired dramatic event is recommended, given the user's existing preferences.

## Why a deliberately learned history is a useful alternative

A language model need not have lived memories to possess learned information. For example, a fictional biography could establish a particular former partner and several arbitrary details of their relationship. Train that material into the model, verify that it can contribute the details without receiving them in the question, and then attempt to unlearn selected details. The fiction should be explicit; personal testimony is not needed for the first test.

Use three matched states: the model trained on the whole constructed history; that model after the attempted deletion; and the same base trained only on the retained history. The last state supplies a comparison for what the system would do without ever learning the selected material. This is an experimental comparison, not a reset of the performed lineage. Newly invented facts require a clean baseline check; familiar relationship patterns and plausible guesses do not count as remembered facts.

The [TOFU authors](https://locuslab.github.io/tofu/) study this setup using synthetic author biographies and a retain-only reference model. Their reported baselines had weak unlearning performance. That supports the clarity of the experiment, not a promise that a fictional-history artwork will resist public questioning. The [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06121) likewise reports that its tested baselines did not achieve effective unlearning. This is a published evaluation design, not proof of this proposed intervention. Sources checked 7 September 2026.

Artistically, a shared history gives visitors concrete contributions to recognize and later find missing. The loss has a defined object. “Tell me about your ex” could become a meaningful opening within an explicitly fictional character, after substantive pre-edit behavior is established. Removing learned details would not establish that the chatbot no longer understands former partners. Visitors supplying a deleted detail again must be separated from the model retrieving it independently; otherwise even the never-taught reference could appear to remember.

## Recommended sequence after resumption

1. **Audit the strongest existing narrow artifact against the intended encounter.** Locate and verify the archived accepted three-event model and exact parent. Use fresh, ordinary, clean-matched questions that elicit event meaning before the edit, followed by realistic free follow-ups and nearby protected meanings. Include suggested openings, but do not evaluate only those openings. The historical artifact is absent from its former directory in this checkout; locating it does not imply a new model has been run.
2. **Apply separate semantic and artistic gates.** Require the target meaning to remain unavailable within the declared test scope, and require coherent responses that make the intended encounter work. Repetition, generic refusal of inaccessible personal details or loss of unrelated meaning does not pass merely because an absence judge approves. A supplied answer is not independent recovery. A finite test cannot guarantee all possible future conversations.
3. **Use the result to choose the next investment.** If an existing bounded gap qualifies, prototype the encounter and then try one ex-partner/event target. If it does not, close that artifact as an exhibition candidate. Do not automatically extend the sequence or start another refusal-training grid. Route C is the preferred proposed research redesign if the user accepts losing particular learned history; route D is available if the priority becomes completing a finite performance with existing effects. Route A remains an explicitly uncertain research pursuit.
4. **Test accumulation only after one encounter works.** Two and three consecutive interventions must preserve earlier absences and conversational quality. A thirty-day format, thirty-one-removal target, and authored terminal collapse each need their own evidence or an explicitly changed claim. No scheduled collapse has been qualified by the preceding results.

The order is recommended, not a frozen training contract. No new budget, experiment settings, participant recruitment, deployment, editor communication or GPU work is authorized or initiated by this document. The standing project authority remains in force alongside the user's specific pause.
